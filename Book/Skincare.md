## Evidence based products
---
Wether it's for making profit or out of common misconceptions, the skincare industry is filled with products and recommendations that are not truly grounded in science. To save you time and money, for each ingredient, always ask yourself:
- has its efficacy been proven?
- is it relevant for my skin in particular?
- what's the appropriate concentration?
- how often should I apply it?
- Is it sensitive to light or heat, and is the packaging designed to protect it accordingly?
- Is it worth the money for its potential benefits?

## Baseline Routine
---
Here is the minimal skincare routine that would benefit anyone, no matter their skin characteristics: 
- **sunscreen every morning.**  It is non-negotiable, UV light damages your skin cells: it makes your skin age faster, and your scars last longer, if not permanently. *Having a skincare without sunscreen is like using a band-aid on a scar you scratch everyday.*
- **washing your face** once (or twice) a day with a **gentle cleanser** helps remove dead cells, pollution, excess sebum, bacterias, sunscreen, and any kind of particule that may clog your pores and cause skin inflammation. *Be careful: if your cleanser is too harsh it may irritate your skin and be counterproductive.*
- **using a moisturizer** after cleaning your face is highly recommended, as it helps to restore the natural skin barrier the cleanser just removed, protecting your skin from dehydration and particules. *Be careful: if your moisturizer is too rich it may clog your pores. Besides you ideally want to use a product close to the natural skin barrier (natural moisturizing factors).*

## Additional actives
---
Additional skincare actives (e.g. serum) may benefit your skin depending on its conditions, "imperfections", or what you want to achieve in general, *with different levels of proof for each active ingredient.*

In my case I like to use niacinamide to balance sebum production, azelaic acid to reduce redness, and retinoids for skin texture and my acne scars.
