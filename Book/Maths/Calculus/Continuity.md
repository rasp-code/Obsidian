A [[Function]] $f$ is **continuous** at a point $a$ if its [[Limit]] at $a$ equals its value at $a$:

$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$$

**Conditions**
- $f$ is defined at $a$.
- $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ exists.
- The limit equals $f(a)$.

$f$ is continuous on a set if it is continuous at every point of that set.

**Types of discontinuity**
- Removable: the limit exists but differs from $f(a)$, or $f(a)$ is undefined.
- Jump: the one-sided limits exist but differ.
- Infinite: at least one one-sided limit is infinite.

**Properties**
- The sum and product of continuous functions are continuous.
- The quotient of continuous functions is continuous where the denominator is nonzero.
- The composition of continuous functions is continuous.
- Intermediate value theorem: if $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, it takes every value between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$.
- Extreme value theorem: if $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, it attains a minimum and a maximum on $[a, b]$.
