A **vector space** over a field of [[Scalar|scalars]] $\mathbb{K}$ (usually $\mathbb{R}$) is a set $V$ equipped with two operations, vector addition $+$ and scalar multiplication $\cdot$, satisfying the axioms below. Its elements are called vectors.

**Axioms for addition** (for all $u, v, w \in V$)
- Associativity: $(u + v) + w = u + (v + w)$.
- Commutativity: $u + v = v + u$.
- Zero vector: there exists $0 \in V$ such that $v + 0 = v$.
- Additive inverse: for each $v$ there exists $-v$ such that $v + (-v) = 0$.

**Axioms for scalar multiplication** (for all $a, b \in \mathbb{K}$ and $u, v \in V$)
- Compatibility: $a(bv) = (ab)v$.
- Identity: $1 \cdot v = v$.
- Distributivity over vector addition: $a(u + v) = au + av$.
- Distributivity over scalar addition: $(a + b)v = av + bv$.

**Examples**
- $\mathbb{R}^n$.
- The set of $m \times n$ real matrices.
- The set of polynomials.
- The set of real-valued functions on a given set.
