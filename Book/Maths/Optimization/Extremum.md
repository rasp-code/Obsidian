An **extremum** of a [[Function]] $f : D \to \mathbb{R}$ is a minimum or a maximum of $f$.

**Definitions** (for $x^* \in D$)
- Minimum: $f(x^*) \leq f(x)$ for the considered points $x$.
- Maximum: $f(x^*) \geq f(x)$ for the considered points $x$.

**Scope**
- Global: the inequality holds for all $x \in D$.
- Local: the inequality holds for all $x \in D$ in some neighborhood of $x^*$.
- Strict: the inequality is strict for $x \neq x^*$.

**Terms**
- $x^*$ is the **extremizer** (minimizer or maximizer).
- $f(x^*)$ is the **extremal value**.

**Properties**
- A maximum of $f$ is a minimum of $-f$.
- The extremal value is unique, but it can be attained at several points.
- An extremum may not exist, e.g. $f(x) = x$ on $\mathbb{R}$.
- Existence: a [[Continuity|continuous]] function on a closed bounded interval attains both a minimum and a maximum.
