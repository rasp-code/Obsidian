A **critical point** (or stationary point) of a differentiable [[Function]] $f : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is a point $\mathbf{x}^*$ where the [[Gradient]] vanishes:

$$\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^*) = 0$$

In one variable: $f'(x^*) = 0$ (see [[Derivative]]).

**Conditions**
- $f$ is differentiable at $\mathbf{x}^*$.
- $\mathbf{x}^*$ is an interior point of the domain.

**Properties**
- Every local [[Extremum]] at an interior point where $f$ is differentiable is a critical point.
- The converse is false: $f(x) = x^3$ has a critical point at $0$ which is not an extremum.
- Geometrically, the tangent hyperplane to the graph is horizontal at a critical point.

**Types of critical points**
- Local minimum.
- Local maximum.
- Saddle point.

**Variant**
- Some definitions also include, in one variable, the points where $f'$ does not exist.
