A **random experiment** is a process whose result cannot be predicted with certainty in advance, but whose set of possible results is known.

**Conditions**
- The procedure is well defined.
- It can, in principle, be repeated under identical conditions.
- All possible results are known before the experiment is carried out.
- Each realization yields exactly one result, called an outcome.
- The result of a single realization is not determined in advance.

**Characteristic**
- Statistical regularity: over many repetitions, the relative frequency of a given result tends to stabilize.

**Examples**
- Rolling a die.
- Drawing a card from a deck.
- Measuring the waiting time before a bus arrives.
