# Description
---
This note describes how I try to manage all the spheres of life as I see it.
It first shows a way of structuring my objectives and duties, then explains how I effectively meet those goals, and finally presents the tools I use to do so.

# Levels of Purpose
---
I like to split my objectives into 3 levels, each defined by what makes it important.
*These levels are ordered by structural dependency, not by value. Level 0 allows the others to hold, but Level 3 is what makes the whole structure worth building.*
## [[Level 0]]: Constraints

This level covers what cannot be compromised, the rules you must play by, which includes financial stability, curative care and secondary/tertiary health prevention, and civic duties. 

*It demands sustained attention, but can be set aside temporarily if it becomes exhausting.*
## [[Level 1]]: Maintenance

This level covers health-maintaining and personal care routines, which includes sleep, nutrition, physical exercice, hygiene, meditation, skincare and haircare.

*It's about caring for yourself as habit, running automatically without polluting the mind.*

(Most of this level is covered by [[Routines|my routines]])
## [[Level 2]]: Pursuits

This level covers anything else; things that are not objectively beneficial for anyone, but which can change everything for you. 

*It is deeply personal and includes everything you like, love, or cherish: **what truly makes life worth living.***

# Getting Things Done
---
To stay organized and meet my objectives, I split everything I want to get done into 7 types of action, based on how each **relates to time**.

## 1. Task

_One-time, no fixed time._

Something to do once, whenever it's convenient (within reason). No deadline, no recurrence, just something on the list until it's done.
**Examples:** make a phone call, send an email, buy a specific item.

## 2. Event

_One-time, fixed time._

Something that happens (or needs to be done) once, at a specific date or time. Unlike a task, timing isn't flexible.
**Examples:** a doctor's appointment, a birthday.

## 3. Habit

_Recurring, no fixed time._

A behavior you want to build into your life, not tied to a particular moment of the day. It's more about staying aware of it continuously until it becomes automatic. This is the hardest type to actually stick to.
**Examples:** walking 10,000 steps a day, drinking water regularly.

## 4. Routine

_Recurring, fixed time (or interval)._

Things to do at a regular interval: daily, weekly, etc. 
Unlike a habit, a routine has a clear rhythm: you know exactly when it's due.
**Examples:** brushing your teeth every day, taking out the trash once a week.

See: [[Routines]]

## 5. Project

_Progressive, no fixed deadline._

A bigger goal made up of several smaller subtasks. You can work on it whenever you want and finish it at your own pace (again, within reason).
**Examples:** making a photo album from vacation, planning a trip.

## 6. Deadline

_Progressive, with a fixed deadline._

Basically a project, except it has to be finished by a specific date.
**Examples:** a school assignment due on a certain day.

## 7. Idea

_No pressure, no commitment._

Something you just jot down as it comes to mind. It's not urgent, not planned, not even confirmed yet, just a seed that might later turn into a task, a project, or something else entirely.

**Examples:** "learn pottery someday", "look into renovating the bathroom".
